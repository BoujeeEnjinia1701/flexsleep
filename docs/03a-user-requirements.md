# FlexSleep Origami Patient Pod user requirements

What the users in FXS-PRB-001 need, written from their side. Priorities are proposals for review with a field hospital team.

Release limitation (STANDARDS 25.5b): no prototype is used in patient care until its stability, fire performance, cleanability and the procedure it embeds are reviewed by qualified IPC and safety staff.

Table 1. User requirements.

| ID | Category | Title | Need | Priority | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| UR-001 | Deployment and assembly | One-person set-up | One of us, with no training and no tools, must set a pod round a cot in under 5 minutes | High | Team deck; volunteer (P6) |
| UR-002 | Performance | Fits our cots | It must fit the field cots and mattresses we have, 60 to 100 cm wide and 190 to 200 cm long | High | Team deck; operator (P1); MSF, NATO and military cot sizes |
| UR-003 | Performance | Privacy lying down | A patient lying in the pod must not be on view to people walking past or to the next bed | High | Patient (P4); shelter manager (P3) |
| UR-004 | Usability | Staff can see the patient | Staff must see every patient without going in, day and night | High | Ward nurse (P2); team deck (semi-transparent upper panels) |
| UR-005 | Performance | Boundary between beds | There must be a physical boundary against touching and against splashes thrown sideways from one bed to the next | High | Team deck; operator (P1) |
| UR-006 | Safety | Access in an emergency | Staff must reach the patient's head and chest within seconds in an arrest, and reach the patient for routine care | High | Ward nurse (P2); resuscitation lead; platform first-order check |
| UR-007 | Safety | Does not fall or give way | The pod must not fall on the patient or anyone else when bumped, leant on or pulled, and must not be mistaken for a handhold | High | Patient (P4); platform first-order check |
| UR-008 | Safety | Fire | A pod round a sleeping patient and bedding must not make a bed fire worse than the site's fire staff accept | High | Site fire safety officer; platform (hardest fire case) |
| UR-009 | Environment and durability | Not too hot | A patient in a warm ward must not overheat inside the pod | Medium | Team risk (heat retention) |
| UR-010 | Maintenance | Cleanable | Every surface must take our disinfectants (0.5 % hypochlorite, 70 % alcohol) between patients | High | IPC lead; team deck |
| UR-011 | Logistics | Packs flat, one carry | The panels must stack to 10 cm or less and one person must carry a pod | Medium | Logistician (P5); team deck |
| UR-012 | Environment and durability | Many uses | It must survive 50 or more deployments | Medium | Team deck; platform |
| UR-013 | Cost | Affordable | About USD 120 a pod, under USD 75 at volume | Medium | Team deck (value-engineering target) |
| UR-014 | Logistics | Ward density | Pods must not cost us many beds in a crowded ward | Medium | Operator (P1); MSF ward space |
| UR-015 | Usability | Not isolating | Patients must not feel shut in | Medium | Team non-technical risk; patient (P4) |
| UR-016 | Regulatory | Honest claims | Nothing on or about the pod may promise infection prevention or a medical function | High | Helpful Engineering; STANDARDS 25.5b |
| UR-017 | Safety | Getting out | A patient must be able to leave, or be taken out, quickly, including in a fire | High | Site fire safety officer; patient (P4) |
